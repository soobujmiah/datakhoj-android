## Why AGPL-3.0 and not MIT

This project was moved from MIT to AGPL-3.0 deliberately.

Under **MIT**, anyone may take this code, strip the author's name, close the
source, publish it on Google Play, and sell it — with no obligation to
contribute anything back. That is a legitimate use of MIT, and it is exactly
what this project does not want.

Under **AGPL-3.0**:

| You may | You must |
|---|---|
| Use it privately, for anything | Keep this copyright notice intact |
| Study and modify the source | Publish your modified source under AGPL-3.0 |
| Share it, including commercially | State clearly what you changed |
| Fork it | Not relicense it as proprietary or MIT |

The key clause is **§13**: if you run a modified version as a *network
service*, you must offer its complete source to every user of that service.
This closes the loophole that lets a company host your work as a SaaS product
without releasing anything.

**In plain terms:** you can learn from this, use it, and build on it. You
cannot take it closed-source and sell it as your own.

Fork the code, not the identity. This is the same rule the Linux kernel,
